<h2><b>What is Autodesk Cloud?</b></h2>
<span style="font-weight: 400;">AutoDesk has been on an uncompromising drive to discontinue all perpetual licensing and move its customer base to subscription licenses. In their own words:-</span>

<b><i>“As of January 2016, Autodesk stopped selling new perpetual licenses. In May 2017, we stopped selling maintenance plans”.</i></b>

<span style="font-weight: 400;">Adding to the confusion, previous products and product bundles have either been discontinued or reconfigured into a new product. This can make the correct management of an Autodesk relationship very complex. Take, for instance, Autodesk Cloud. With other vendors, a cloud subscription is a straightforward alternative to an on-premise solution. While Autodesk does have cloud solutions for Building and construction, like BIM 360, and Manufacturing, like Fusion 360, some of their cloud offerings are additional products that require an existing subscription or maintenance agreement for related products to be in place before one can acquire the product. For example, </span><a href="https://www.autodesk.com/products/rendering/overview"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Autodesk Rendering</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> is </span>

<b><i>“Available only to subscribers and maintenance plan customers of select Autodesk software”</i></b>

<span style="font-weight: 400;">What adds to the complexity is that to use Autodesk Rendering, you also need Cloud Credits. For an individual or a small business, cloud credits are fairly easy to manage. For a larger organization, more administration has to be done to manage the cloud credits and assign entitlement to the appropriate users.</span>

<span style="font-weight: 400;">As of July 2019, the following </span><a href="https://www.autodesk.com/360-cloud"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Autodesk Cloud products</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> are available:-</span>
<ul>
 <li style="font-weight: 400;"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Collaboration Products - AutoCAD Mobile App and Recap (Reality Capture) Pro</span></li>
 <li style="font-weight: 400;"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Building and Construction - BIM 360, Formit (sketcher), and plug-in </span><a href="https://www.autodesk.com/products/insight/overview"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Insight</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> (building performance analysis)</span></li>
 <li style="font-weight: 400;"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Product Design and Manufacturing - </span><a href="https://www.autodesk.com/products/fusion-360/overview#banner"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Fusion 360</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">, Fusion Team and Fusion Lifecycle, as well as Configurator 360</span></li>

<h3><b>What are Cloud Credits from Autodesk?</b></h3>
<span style="font-weight: 400;">Cloud credits are a form of consumption token used to “pay” for some of Autodesk’s cloud services, rather like, but different from Token-Flex tokens. Cloud credits are provided on purchase of some Autodesk products, such as Revit, or are purchased directly or as a top-up from the Autodesk Store. All cloud credits are not the same however; the credits you get with your software purchase are known as “starter” cloud credits, while those purchased from the store are “purchased” credits. 100 starter credits are a “gift” from Autodesk (their words) and are non-transferable, only to be used by the named user, while purchased credits can be shared.</span>

<span style="font-weight: 400;">A cloud credit has a maximum 12-month lifespan, after which it expires, but all credits expire when the current contract expires, so care must be taken when topping up.</span>
